Does the neighborhood environment matter for children's mental health?

What we found

Children in neighborhoods perceived as safer, more walkable and socially connected tended to have fewer emotional or behavioral difficulties and stronger social competence.

How the study was conducted

Questionnaire data for 695 fourth graders, with adjustment for family socioeconomic factors.

Why it matters

Neighborhood improvements may be relevant to efforts to reduce child health inequalities.

How to interpret the findings
Perceived environments and observational data cannot establish causation.

Finding at a glance

Children in neighborhoods perceived as safer, more walkable and socially connected tended to have fewer emotional or behavioral difficulties and stronger social competence.
