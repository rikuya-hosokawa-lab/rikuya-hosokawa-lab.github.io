Is posting on social media related to university students’ eating patterns?

What we found

Students who posted on social media were more likely to eat late at night than those who only browsed; breakfast skipping did not differ significantly by posting. Longer SNS use was associated with both breakfast skipping and late-night eating.

Adjusted risk ratio for late-night eating, posting versus browsing only: 1.21 (95% CI 1.14–1.29). This cross-sectional estimate does not establish prospective risk.

How the study was conducted

Adjusted cross-sectional analysis of 21,780 student survey responses.

Why it matters

Consider social media use together with daily routines in health education.

How to interpret the findings
Public abstract summary; causation is unknown.
