How are fathers’ involvement and family economic status related to preschool social skills?

What we found

In two-parent households, greater paternal involvement and higher family economic status were associated with stronger assertion, self-control and cooperation in preschool children.

How the study was conducted

The study included 1,341 Japanese children aged four to five; mothers reported family circumstances and involvement, and childcare professionals assessed social skills.

Why it matters

The findings can inform support for families and opportunities for caregivers to participate in childcare.

How to interpret the findings
Cross-sectional associations do not establish causality or imply that any family structure is superior.

Key finding

In two-parent households, greater paternal involvement and higher family economic status were associated with stronger assertion, self-control and cooperation in preschool children.
