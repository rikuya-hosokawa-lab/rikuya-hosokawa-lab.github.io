Does Second Step help preschoolers build social-emotional skills?

What we found

Children who took part had better assertiveness, self-control and cooperation scores and fewer behavioral problems than comparison children after the program.

How the study was conducted

Twenty-five classroom sessions for 4–5-year-olds, with pre/post and comparison-group assessment.

Why it matters

Early learning can provide opportunities to practice emotions and interpersonal skills.

How to interpret the findings
Nonrandomized intervention; long-term outcomes need further study.

Finding at a glance

Children who took part had better assertiveness, self-control and cooperation scores and fewer behavioral problems than comparison children after the program.
