Do early social skills relate to children's later mental health?

What we found

Children with stronger social skills before school entry tended to have fewer emotional and behavioral problems after entering school, even accounting for earlier problems.

How the study was conducted

One-year follow-up of 1,604 children, with parent and teacher assessments and path analysis.

Why it matters

Both family support and opportunities to develop social skills may be important.

How to interpret the findings
Longitudinal associations are not proof of intervention effectiveness.

Finding at a glance

Children with stronger social skills before school entry tended to have fewer emotional and behavioral problems after entering school, even accounting for earlier problems.
