MESSAGE

To prospective graduate students

My experience as a public health nurse taught me that health is shaped not only by individual choices but also by families, neighborhoods and social structures. My research in public health nursing and life-course epidemiology explores the environments that support health from childhood to older age.

Graduate study offers an opportunity to turn questions from daily practice into research questions and investigate them with data. I value sharing research through publications and applying findings to public health practice, education and policy.

You do not need a fully developed research topic to start a conversation.

If you are curious about a health issue, want to investigate a challenge from practice or hope to develop research skills, please get in touch. Early enquiries are welcome.

LEARNING & RESEARCH

Skills to develop through graduate study

Formulate research questions

Review the evidence and develop research objectives and methods from practical and social challenges.

Investigate questions with data

Build skills in epidemiology, statistical analysis, regional data and program evaluation.

Communicate research findings

Share results through publications, conference presentations and clear visual summaries.

Connect research with practice

Consider how evidence can inform public health, nursing, education and policy.

Enquiries are welcome from public health nurses, nurses, other professionals and people interested in further study in epidemiology and public health.

Q & A

Frequently asked questions

Can I get in touch before choosing a research topic?

Yes. I welcome preliminary enquiries about your interests and questions from practice, even if your research proposal is not yet developed.

Can I consider graduate study while working as a nurse or public health nurse?

Please contact me about research drawing on your professional experience. Work–study arrangements and course options depend on individual circumstances and university regulations.

Can I enquire if I have limited experience in statistics or academic English?

Yes. Tell me what you are interested in learning. We can discuss suitable research methods and preparation.

What topics can I discuss?

Healthy life expectancy, care prevention, community health, child and family health, health inequalities and related public health questions are among the areas of interest.

What should I include in my first email?

Your name, current affiliation or experience, interests and intended timing are helpful, as far as you know them. A completed proposal is not needed for an initial enquiry.

How do I apply and confirm supervision?

Please follow the university’s official application and pre-application consultation procedures. See the admissions links below for current details.
